- (direction) Source of truth is the complete brainstorming conversation; do not assume or cite an uploaded proposal. User requested a reviewable initial specification and no implementation.
- (capability) CAP-1 intent: Donors, sponsors, and camp managers manage their authorized profiles and camp participation/sponsorship records. success: Synthetic donor profiles, preferences, camp registrations, sponsor offers, and camp-linked sponsorship activity can be created and viewed only by permitted roles; no payment transactions occur.
- (capability) CAP-2 intent: Blood bank staff record donations and screening status so unit records reflect the authorized operational outcome. success: A technician can record a donation and screening status; pending or failed units are excluded from available stock and clinical eligibility remains a qualified human decision.
- (capability) CAP-3 intent: Staff maintain traceable blood-unit inventory and availability. success: Each synthetic unit has a unique ID, blood group, component, volume, collection/expiry dates, location, screening and lifecycle status; stock summaries and expiry/low-stock alerts exclude pending, failed, reserved, issued, discarded, or expired units.
- (capability) CAP-4 intent: Authorized hospital requesters submit and track requests while blood bank staff reserve and issue units through controlled workflows. success: Request status and requested/reserved/issued/remaining quantities are visible to the requesting hospital; high priority precedes standard priority with FIFO within each tier; reservations follow FEFO among staff-approved candidates and concurrent actions cannot allocate one unit twice.
- (capability) CAP-5 intent: Authorized medical staff identify donor candidates and send call-ups while donors control contact preferences. success: Candidate filtering uses an explicitly identified rule set for the synthetic prototype, sends only through the simulated SMS gateway, and honors recorded opt-outs; the system does not make final medical eligibility decisions.
- (capability) CAP-6 intent: Users perform only the functions and access the records allowed by their role and organization. success: All eight roles authenticate; server-side authorization isolates bank/hospital data; sensitive changes retain actor, timestamp, old/new values, reason, and record ID in an auditable history.
- (capability) CAP-7 intent: Managers and staff monitor operations and risks through basic dashboards and alerts. success: Dashboards show current synthetic stock and donation summaries, with low-stock and expiry warnings derived from current unit state.
- (constraint) The v1 is a four-member university-team prototype using a familiar technology, centralized PostgreSQL, synthetic data, and simulated multi-bank/hospital contexts; live SMS and external hospital/NBTS/laboratory integrations are excluded.
- (constraint) No real patient, donor, or screening data, no actual healthcare-facility blood issuing, no patient names, and no automated clinical compatibility or final donor-eligibility decisions; qualified staff retain clinical responsibility.
- (constraint) Medical eligibility and shelf-life rules vary by source; prototype rules must be clearly identified and versioned, and NBTS confirmation plus Sri Lankan legal/privacy review are required before real deployment.
- (constraint) The system is responsive and assumes internet access for v1; team technology familiarity, limited access to 2-3 interviews, and an unspecified timeline constrain validation and delivery.
- (constraint) Donor SMS consent/preferences and opt-out must be respected; hospital users see availability counts and their own requests, not donor identities; sponsorship records do not process payments.
- (direction) Donor role: self-registers, preregisters for camps, views own history and future camps, updates contact and SMS preferences, and responds to call-ups.
- (direction) Sponsor role: person or organization registers, views camps, offers support, and tracks sponsorship activities and camp-linked contributions; no payment processing.
- (direction) Hospital Requester role: authorized hospital representative submits requests, views availability counts, and tracks only their hospital request status and fulfillment.
- (direction) Medical Officer In-Charge role: maintains donor records, approves camps, manages donor call-up campaigns, filters candidate donors, and sends simulated call-ups; final medical decisions remain human.
- (direction) Blood Bank Technician role: records donations and screening status, registers units, reserves and issues approved requests after checks and confirmation, records deferrals and discard reasons, and processes request queues.
- (direction) Camp Manager role: plans camps, manages donor registrations, sends simulated SMS, records camp outcomes, and manages camp sponsors.
- (direction) Blood Bank Manager role: monitors dashboard and risks, reviews reports, oversees stock and authorizes transfers; under proposed rules also authorizes priority overrides and exceptional operational releases.
- (direction) Admin role: manages user accounts, hospital/blood-bank master records, settings and rule configuration, and views audit history; does not decide clinical eligibility or individual request urgency.
- (decision) MoSCoW: Must—eight-role authentication/RBAC, donor and basic camp/sponsor workflows, donation/screening, unit lifecycle and accurate stock, alerts, requests, FEFO reservation and confirmed prototype issue, candidate filtering and simulated call-ups, basic dashboards, validation, audit, transaction safety. Should—pre-registration, advanced camp/sponsor features, deferrals, transfers, notifications, detailed reports/exports, configurable rules, improved accessibility/localization. Could—QR/barcodes, offline capture, advanced analytics, optional multilingual pages, additional sponsorship. Won’t—live SMS/integrations, real medical data, payment processing, automated clinical decisions, ML, and actual healthcare-facility issuing.
- (decision) Priority proposal: authorized Hospital Requester sets initial urgency; Blood Bank Manager may override with reason and audit; high-priority requests precede standard requests with FIFO within each tier, and partial remainder remains active and high priority.
- (decision) Unit release proposal: technician may issue after required checks and a second confirmation; Blood Bank Manager authorizes exceptional operational overrides; failed screening, expiry, and other hard safety restrictions cannot be overridden.
- (constraint) Critical record corrections are role-restricted and auditable with record ID, old/new values, actor, timestamp, and reason; preserve issue/discard history, notify affected staff/hospital when needed, and do not permanently delete critical records.
- (constraint) Concurrent reservation and issue must use atomic validation/transactions; recheck current availability, screening status, and expiry at confirmation; prevent duplicate allocation and provide a clear recoverable error.
- (assumption) V1 eligibility and shelf-life filters use clearly identified synthetic demonstration rules; configurable rule editing/version history is Should-have, and no demo rule is suitable for real clinical use without NBTS confirmation.
- (assumption) The prototype simulates multiple hospitals and blood banks for access-isolation and transfer testing, without connecting to real institutions or issuing real units.
- (question) What does second confirmation mean for issue: a second explicit confirmation by the technician, or an independent confirmation by a second authorized person?
- (question) What reservation expiry duration and release authority should apply, and may the system perform an atomic audited release only when the unit remains unissued?
- (question) Which demo eligibility and shelf-life rules and sources are approved, and which actor approves the recorded screening result for unit release?
- (question) What retention and deletion rules apply to donor/contact/screening data and audit history after Sri Lankan legal and NBTS review?
- (question) What exact sponsorship fields should be recorded for offers or in-kind/financial commitments without processing payments?
- (direction) Recommended delivery order: foundation with synthetic data, institution scoping, roles/auth/audit; donor/camp/sponsor basics; donation/screening/unit states; stock/alerts and transactional FEFO request/reservation/issue; simulated SMS and dashboards; Should/Could features; then role-isolation, safety, race, privacy, and backup/restore acceptance checks.
- (direction) Preserve donor responses to call-ups, camp notifications, donor deferral records and reasons, and failure cases for duplicate donor/camp/request records, interval violations, unauthorized camp approval, cancellation notices, SMS limits/responses, configuration/scheduled-job failures, and stale or missing alerts/exports.
- (constraint) English is the v1 language; Sinhala/Tamil donor-facing pages and SMS templates are optional. Real-value donor rewards and paid blood handling are out of scope. Validation is limited to 2-3 supervisor-arranged interviews and published sources; emergency-response timing targets are unspecified.
- (direction) Recommended review matrix: donors and sponsors access own records; hospitals access their requests and aggregate stock only; medical officers manage donor records/candidate filters/camp approvals; technicians manage donation/screening/unit operations; camp managers manage camps and sponsorship links; blood-bank managers oversee stock/transfers and proposed overrides; admins manage accounts/master/configuration/audit but make no clinical or per-request priority decisions. Exact grants remain open.

- (event) Preservation validation pass: conversation requirements, eight roles, MoSCoW priorities, workflows, business rules, safety controls, failure modes, and remaining gaps are represented in SPEC.md or its initial-project-specification.md companion.
- (direction) Recommended state model for review: unit Registered/PendingScreening becomes Available only after required data and recorded pass; Available becomes Reserved then Issued; discard is explicit with reason; expiry is derived; transfers use in-transit/received states. Requests move from Submitted/Review to Approved or waiting stock, Reserved/PartiallyReserved, PartiallyIssued, and Fulfilled, with Rejected/Cancelled branches. Active reservations resolve to Issued or an explicit audited expiry/cancellation release; exact names, cancellation semantics, and expiry timing remain open.
- (constraint) Demonstration results use synthetic data and cannot establish real-world clinical or operational improvement; failure cases include incorrect/incomplete requests and an issue attempted without required confirmation, which must not commit.

- (direction) User approved independent specification refinement and resolution of project-level gaps; preserve the brainstorming conversation as source, do not code or create system architecture, and retain CAP-1 through CAP-7.
- (decision) Remove PostgreSQL and replaceable-gateway architecture as committed constraints; leave stack selection to the team and specify only a simulated SMS behavior contract.
- (decision) Adopt the least-privilege eight-role permission matrix as the v1 access contract; staff accounts are institution-scoped and every read, mutation, dashboard, audit view, and export is authorized server-side.
- (decision) Define camp lifecycle as Draft, PendingApproval, Published, Full, Completed, or Cancelled; Camp Manager manages camps and Medical Officer In-Charge approves publication as a project-level recommendation, not external policy.
- (decision) Define unit lifecycle with PendingScreening, Available, Reserved, Issued, Discarded, Expired, and Quarantined; no hard-blocked or terminal unit can return to Available, and every unit links to its source donation.
- (assumption) For synthetic prototype records only, a Blood Bank Technician records the screening status supplied for the scenario; this does not establish real-world screening authority or clinical validation.
- (decision) Require two distinct authorized technician accounts for prototype issue initiation and confirmation; manager approvals may alter operational priority or release a still-valid unissued reservation but never bypass safety blocks.
- (assumption) Use a 30-minute reservation lifetime as a synthetic prototype default; expiry is an audited atomic transition and does not authorize release of expired, failed, discarded, or quarantined stock.
- (decision) Define request states, request ownership, cancellation limits, and positive integer quantity invariants; no patient name or identifying clinical narrative is accepted in a request.
- (decision) Use High and Standard urgency; order by urgency then immutable server submission time and request ID, preserve original queue time for partial fulfillment, and audit manager priority overrides.
- (decision) Treat donor filtering as candidate support only; require human review before call-up, enforce consent and do-not-contact at send time, and exclude any recorded active deferral without computing final medical eligibility.
- (decision) Define simulated message states and idempotent retry behavior, retain donor responses, and prohibit sensitive screening or patient details in message content.
- (decision) Define sponsorship tracking as camp-linked offers with proposed, accepted, declined, withdrawn, and fulfilled states; record non-payment descriptions and quantities only, with no payment execution or transfer of funds.
- (constraint) The v1 rule set is seeded, synthetic, source-labelled, and not configurable through an admin editor; configurable versioned rules remain Should Have and no demo value is a clinical recommendation.
- (decision) Treat critical state changes and their append-only audit events as one transaction; record actor, scope, timestamp, entity, action, reason, outcome, and minimal old/new values while redacting sensitive data.
- (decision) Define synthetic-only identity controls including admin-provisioned staff accounts, donor/sponsor self-registration, secure credential storage, session expiry, account disablement, login throttling, and server-side authorization.
- (decision) Specify UTC event timestamps and Asia/Colombo display; expiry uses a timestamp and expires when current time is at or after expiresAt, while values come only from labelled synthetic rules.
- (decision) Add data lineage and validation across donors, camps, donations, screening events, units, requests, reservations, issues, sponsors, messages, institutions, and audit events without prescribing database technology or API architecture.
- (decision) Require a documented synthetic backup/restore test that verifies inventory, requests, reservations, and audit history together; do not claim production recovery objectives.
- (question) Before any real-world use, authoritative NBTS/institutional review must approve screening authority, eligibility and shelf-life rules, component handling, and operational issue policy; these are not inferred by the prototype specification.
- (question) Before any use of real personal data, legal/privacy review must determine lawful basis, notices/consent, retention/deletion, access rights, breach handling, and applicable Sri Lankan obligations.
- (event) Specification refinement decisions recorded before deriving the canonical kernel and detailed companion; all unconfirmed clinical, legal, and institutional matters remain external validation gates.
- (decision) Use status labels confirmed, recommendation adopted, prototype assumption, and external validation gate so conversation requirements remain distinct from later project-level decisions.
- (decision) Request lifecycle is Submitted, UnderReview, WaitingForStock, PartiallyReserved, Reserved, PartiallyIssued, Fulfilled, Rejected, CancellationRequested, Cancelled, or ClosedPartiallyFulfilled; requester may cancel before allocation and cancellation after allocation releases only unissued valid stock.
- (decision) Campaign recipients are human-reviewed candidates; every delivery attempt checks consent and suppression at send time, deduplicates per campaign/donor, records state, and uses no sensitive medical content.
- (constraint) Hospitals see aggregate counts only, never unit identifiers, donor identity, screening details, or patient names; minimum staff access is scoped to assigned institutions and roles.
- (decision) Basic sponsor records capture sponsor, camp, non-payment offer description, quantity or delivery details, status, dates, and audit history; no money amount or payment workflow is required in v1.
- (constraint) No fixed production availability, response-time, retention, recovery-time, or recovery-point objective is promised; emergency response remains a human operational responsibility.
- (direction) Detailed companion will contain MoSCoW requirements with traceable IDs, role permissions, state transitions, workflows, data fields and relationships, validations, security/privacy controls, exception handling, and executable acceptance outcomes.
- (event) Pre-render decision log completed; the detailed specification remains a requirements contract and does not select a technology stack, API design, database schema, or delivery plan.
- (decision) Refined state tables to permit only audited quarantine-to-pending revalidation, expire all nonterminal units at expiry, permit discard of blocked units with reason, and fulfill requests only through committed issue.
- (decision) Camp Full is not a base state; expose capacity/full behavior only when the Should-have preregistration/capacity feature is enabled. Simulated delivery status is named SimulatedDelivered to distinguish it from real SMS.
- (decision) Align v1 donor filtering with Should deferral management: Must includes consent, opt-out, human review, and a minimal Medical-Officer-managed call-up suppression flag; detailed deferral history/reasons remain Should.
- (decision) V1 account recovery is Admin-initiated for synthetic accounts and resets credentials without revealing them; no live email or SMS recovery integration is required.
- (event) Coherence sweep resolved the Must candidate-filter versus Should deferral conflict, aligned simulated message status names, and constrained request fulfillment/camp registration acceptance paths.
- (decision) Basic manager dashboards include request counts by urgency and waiting time to reveal backlog without automatically changing priority or promising response times.
- (decision) Should-have features have conditional acceptance criteria; they are tested only if selected for implementation, while Could features require separate scope approval and acceptance criteria.
- (decision) Fail closed when no identified synthetic rule set is active: do not produce an eligibility result or campaign recipient set, and do not make a unit Available without a valid expiry rule version; rule changes never silently rewrite prior outcomes.
