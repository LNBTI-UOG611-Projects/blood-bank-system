- (technique) started Role Playing
- (idea by coach) As a blood bank manager, I need a trustworthy live view of usable units by blood group, component, location, and expiry, with reserved units clearly separated from available stock.
- (direction by user) In scope: manage donors, camps, donations, screening status, blood units, hospital requests, issues, and staff; stock summaries by blood group and component; expiry/low-stock warnings; eligible-donor lists; unit lifecycle, FEFO reservation, confirmed issue; simulated SMS call-up, notifications, dashboards, reports with CSV/PDF export; RBAC, audit log, configurable rules with version history; responsive mobile-friendly English UI, with Sinhala/Tamil donor pages and SMS templates if time allows.
- (insight by user) Target roles: technician (donations, screening, unit registration, reserve/issue); donor coordinator (donors, camps, eligible lists, call-ups); manager (stock, risks, transfer approval, reports); hospital requester (requests, status, availability counts); donor (camps, own history, contact details, call-ups); administrator (accounts, master data, rules, audit log).
- (direction by user) Boundary: end-to-end from donor registration/camp through donation, screening status, unit registration, stock, reservation, issue and outcome, plus risk monitoring/call-ups. Hospitals only request and see availability counts; patients are not users and patient names are never stored. SMS is simulated; NBTS and hospital information systems are external.
- (direction by user) Out of scope: clinical compatibility decisions and medical eligibility judgments; lab instrument or real hospital/national-system integrations; real donor/patient/screening data; payments, real-value rewards, paid blood handling, and ML.
- (insight by user) Constraints: synthetic data cannot establish real-world impact; published medical rules differ and must be configurable and confirmed with NBTS before real use; timeline unknown, so use MoSCoW; real SMS optional, internet assumed, offline camp capture Should-have; choose familiar technology; only 2-3 supervisor-arranged interviews plus published sources; review Sri Lankan data protection law and NBTS policies before deployment.
- (idea by coach) As a hospital requester, I would want each request to show a clear status and the quantity still pending, especially when only part of the request can be fulfilled, without exposing patient names or donor details.
- (idea by coach) Proposal for hospital request tracking: Submitted, Under review, Awaiting stock/Partially reserved, Ready for issue, Partially issued, Fulfilled, with Cancelled/Rejected branches; always show requested, reserved, issued, and remaining quantities, last update, and next action or operational reason. Do not promise an ETA without reliable data, and do not expose patient or donor identity.
- (idea by user) If a hospital request is only partially fulfilled, keep the remaining quantity active, give it high priority, and fulfill it as soon as possible.
- (idea by coach) A partial request could retain a visible high-priority follow-up flag and alert authorized staff until the remaining quantity is resolved; this is workflow escalation, not automated clinical prioritization.
- (direction by user) Queue allocation should follow FIFO order, while considering priority; absent a high-priority request, serve requests in order.
- (decision by user) Queue rule: high-priority requests precede regular requests, and FIFO applies within each priority level.
- (idea by coach) For queue transparency, show original request time, current priority, remaining quantity, and fulfillment progress; log priority changes and the staff member/action for audit.
- (direction by user) User suggests hospital and admin involvement in priority setting and asks for a careful role design because mistakes could cost lives.
- (idea by coach) RBAC proposal: authorized hospital clinician/request approver can declare urgency; blood bank manager oversees queue handling and documented exceptions; system administrator manages accounts/configuration but cannot change individual request urgency. Log every priority change with actor, timestamp, and reason; system ranks declared priority then FIFO, without making clinical decisions. Validate real-world policy with hospitals/NBTS before deployment.
- (question by user) Asked for the recommended contents of priority-change records.
- (idea by coach) Priority audit recommendation: capture the actor and role, timestamp, prior/new priority, and a required non-identifying reason code from a hospital/NBTS-approved list; avoid patient identifiers and free-text clinical narratives. Alert blood-bank staff promptly and do not let an administrative approval step silently delay care; validate workflow with stakeholders before real use.
- (direction by user) Cover the whole system, not just technician perspective. Technician needs donation and screening recording, unit registration, reservation/issue, outcomes, reasoned discards, queue processing, and other capabilities needed in a complete real-world system.
- (idea by coach) For safe unit lifecycle control, keep pending/unreleased units visibly quarantined and out of reservable/issuable stock; require authorized staff to record release status, track every unit status change, and capture discard reason. The app records and enforces workflow state but does not interpret clinical/lab results.
- (direction by user) Expanded roles to eight: Donor self-registers, preregisters for camps, views history/camps, manages SMS preferences, responds to call-ups; Sponsor registers, views camps, offers support, tracks sponsorships/contributions; Hospital Requester submits requests, tracks pending/approved/processed status, and views availability; Medical Officer In-Charge maintains donor records, approves camps, manages call-up campaigns, identifies eligible donors, sends SMS; Blood Bank Technician records donations/screening, registers units, reserves and issues approved requests, records deferrals and reasoned discards; Camp Manager plans camps, manages donor registrations, sends simulated SMS, records outcomes, manages sponsors; Blood Bank Manager monitors dashboard/alerts, reports, approves transfers; Admin manages accounts, blood banks/hospitals, settings/rules, audit log.
- (insight by user) Additional system functions: simulated SMS gateway; reserve units is included in issuing; find eligible donors is included in sending call-ups; login/logout are common to all eight roles, not separate use cases. Overall system centralizes donors, donations, stock, requests, camps, and sponsorship.
- (question by coach) Scope ambiguity to resolve: sponsors can offer support and track contributions, while payments are explicitly out of scope; decide whether sponsorship tracking records pledges/offers only with no financial transactions.
- (question by coach) Unit release authority remains unspecified: user lists technicians entering screening status but has not named who confirms release for reservation/issue.
- (direction by user) Sponsorship module: individuals/organizations register, view upcoming camps, offer sponsorships, and track activities; Camp Manager manages sponsors and camp-linked sponsorship records. This clarifies activity coordination; payment processing remains outside the stated scope.
- (technique) finished Role Playing; switched to First Principles Thinking
- (idea by coach) First-principles candidate: availability should be derived from a uniquely identified unit with an authorized released status and a current, traceable lifecycle state; reservations, issues, discards, expiry, or missing/conflicting records must not silently inflate usable-stock counts.
- (idea by user) Available-unit minimums: unique unit ID; blood group; component; volume; collection date; expiry computed from configured component shelf life; storage location; passed screening; and unit status Available. Pending/failed screening and Reserved/Issued/Discarded/Expired statuses are excluded.
- (idea by coach) For traceability, store which version of the configured shelf-life rule produced each expiry date; if required rule/data is missing or conflicting, do not silently count the unit as available and surface it for staff correction.
- (direction by user) Only authorized roles may resolve data conflicts: technicians correct donations, screening, and unit details; managers handle stock, transfers, and critical inventory; admins handle accounts, system settings, and master data. Audit every correction with record ID, old/new values, actor, timestamp, and reason; preserve traceability, prohibit permanent deletion of critical records, and enforce RBAC.
- (idea by coach) Treat corrections as traceable events rather than silent overwrites; keep original values and actor/time/reason, and use a compensating correction workflow for records already tied to stock or request activity.
- (direction by user) For post-action unit corrections, restrict permissions and preserve history; technicians may request/perform permitted edits, managers review/approve critical unit or stock corrections. For reserved units, check reservation impact and notify technician/manager; for issued units, preserve issue and notify manager and hospital requester when affected; for discarded units, retain discard status and disposal record. Audit unit ID, old/new values, reason, user, timestamp; never auto-return reserved/issued/discarded units to Available; apply only permitted status transitions.
- (technique) finished First Principles Thinking; switched to Reverse Brainstorming
- (idea by coach) Reverse brainstorm failure seed: make inventory unsafe by silently changing a unit status or counting a reserved, expired, issued, or discarded unit as available, with no audit alert.
- (idea by user) Critical race-condition failure: two technicians reserve/issue the same unit concurrently, creating duplicate allocation, inaccurate stock, and delayed requests. Require an atomic transaction and fresh checks of availability, screening pass, and expiry before confirmation; reject stale/unavailable selections clearly, allow another unit selection, audit successful actions, and update stock immediately.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—donor/unit data: duplicates or incorrect group, component, collection date, screening status, or location produce misleading stock and donor lists.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—rule changes: an unversioned shelf-life or eligibility-rule update silently changes existing classifications or expiry dates.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—requests: cancellation or partial fulfillment leaves reservations stuck, loses the remainder, or breaks the priority/FIFO order.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—donor communications: opt-outs are ignored, messages go to the wrong or outdated contact, failed SMS is shown as sent, or a call-up is sent using stale eligibility information.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—camps: capacity is overbooked, changed location/time is not communicated, registrations are duplicated, or outcomes/attendance are lost.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—privacy/RBAC: a user sees another hospital’s requests or donor screening details, patient-identifying information is entered, or audit history can be altered without trace.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—transfers/discards: a transfer is counted at both locations or neither, a discard can be reversed accidentally, or the disposal reason and approver are missing.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—offline/reliability: camp records are lost or duplicated during later sync, an outage blocks safe work without a recovery path, or backups cannot be restored.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—reports/exports: stale figures or a CSV/PDF export expose records the requesting role should not see.
- (idea by coach) Failure mode—sponsorship: an offer or contribution is attached to the wrong camp or counted twice, confusing sponsor and camp records.
- (insight by user) Cross-system risks include duplicate/misclassified/expired or un-screened units, invalid transitions, double reservations, stock drift; duplicate/inaccurate/unauthorized/delayed requests and incomplete transfers; duplicate/ineligible/deferred donors, interval violations, wrong contacts or ignored opt-outs; camp scheduling/approval/registration/cancellation/outcome errors; duplicate or mislinked sponsorship; SMS delivery/recipient/duplication/limit/response failures; RBAC/privilege/privacy/critical-record changes; outages, incomplete transactions, concurrency, data/backup/network/config/scheduled-job failures; inaccurate dashboards, missing alerts, bad reports/exports, incomplete audit.
- (direction by user) Mitigations: server-side RBAC, validation, transactions for critical inventory operations, no duplicate allocation, permitted status transitions, accurate stock, clear errors, audit retention, sensitive-data protection, backup/recovery, and alerts to authorized users on critical failures. Qualified medical staff retain final donor-eligibility and compatibility decisions; system provides reliable information only.
